RV Lemon Law in Houston, TX

We review RV repair records and warranty complaints from Houston-area owners.

Call (832) 245-5128 Ask a question

Owners call us after saying, "The slide-out will not come back in," "The RV keeps going to the shop," or "They fixed it, but the same warning light returned." Those words often describe a repair history that needs more than another service appointment. We translate the owner’s account into the repair orders, dates, mileage entries, and warranty records connected to the RV.

A motorhome, travel trailer, and fifth wheel can each develop different problems, but the paperwork often follows the same path. We look at whether the service center recorded the complaint, what the technician wrote about the inspection, what repair was attempted, and whether the owner returned with the same condition. A roof leak, leveling jack failure, generator shutdown, or electrical fault can leave a different trail in the service file.

An RV concern is worth looking at now when repeat repairs, shop stays, or an unresolved warranty complaint appear in the records. Repair-order language can become harder to reconstruct after service visits pass, and the date a condition was first reported may matter when we compare it with the warranty documents.

Slide-out stuck, warning light on

We hear owners describe a slide-out that will not extend or retract, leveling jacks that drop or refuse to lift, a generator that starts and then quits, and water that enters through a roof seam or window frame after rain. Other callers report a check-engine light, intermittent power at the outlets, a refrigerator that stops cooling, or a brake controller that keeps displaying a fault message. The words used during a call matter because a service writer may have recorded "electrical concern" while the owner reported a loss of power only after the RV warmed up. Similar-sounding driveway complaints can lead to very different records, repairs, warranty questions, and next steps once we read the actual work orders.

Repair-order file review

We begin a file review by discussing the RV type, whether it was purchased or leased, the main condition that brought it to the shop, and the repair visits the owner remembers. That first conversation usually settles the basic timeline, the service location, and whether the complaint involves the coach, chassis, engine, plumbing, electrical equipment, or a slide-out. Exact wording, mileage, shop dates, and warranty reporting dates need the paperwork in front of us.

We then read the purchase or lease agreement, each repair order, and the dates the RV went into and came out of the shop. We compare the owner’s stated complaint with the technician notes, parts listed, diagnosis, and completed repair line on each order. We also review warranty documents and correspondence with the dealer or service department, then identify records that may need to be gathered before we can assess the file further.

Repair attempts and warranty dates

Repeated repair attempts carry more weight when each visit documents the same symptom or a closely connected component. A work order that says the slide-out bound during operation, for example, gives us more to compare than a general note saying the RV was checked. We look for the complaint date, the technician’s findings, the parts installed, and whether the owner returned because the same equipment failed again.

Days out of service also matter because the in-and-out dates show how long the RV stayed with the dealer or repair facility. We check whether the fault was reported while the warranty was active, whether the RV was bought or leased, and whether the owner has the original records or must locate them through a dealer, lender, or service center. A complete file does not decide every question, but repair orders, warranty dates, and documented downtime give us a clearer basis for evaluating the matter.

Repair orders and VIN

Before calling us, owners can gather repair orders from the RV dealer, service center, and any authorized repair facility. Each order should show the date, mileage or usage reading, reported complaint, technician notes, and the date the RV was released. We also use the purchase or lease agreement, which often identifies the selling dealer, delivery date, and vehicle description. The VIN usually appears on the registration, insurance card, purchase papers, or the RV’s identification label, and motorhomes may have separate coach and chassis paperwork.
