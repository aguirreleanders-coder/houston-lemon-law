RV Lemon Law in Houston, TX

We review recurring RV defects, repair orders, warranty records, and shop visits.

Call (832) 245-5128 Ask a question

Owners call us after saying things like, “The slide-out keeps getting stuck,” “We cannot use the RV after every shop visit,” or “The warning light came back before the next trip.” Those descriptions often point to a recurring defect, repeated repair work, or an RV that has spent too much time at the service department.

We hear from motorhome and trailer owners in Houston, West University Place, Southside Place, and Bellaire who have returned with the same electrical fault, water leak, engine concern, leveling problem, or appliance failure. The first conversation helps us separate a one-time repair from a problem that continued after the dealer or authorized shop worked on it.

An RV concern is worth looking at now because repair orders, warranty status, mileage entries, and shop dates can become harder to reconstruct after additional trips or ownership changes. We can begin by reviewing the documents that show what happened at each visit.

Slide-out failures and warning lights

Owners often report that the slide-out will not extend, will not retract, or stops halfway and leaves the living area unusable. Others describe a dashboard warning light that returns after a repair, an engine that loses power on the road, or a generator that shuts down under load.

Water intrusion brings another set of calls: a soft floor near a wall, a ceiling stain around a vent, damp cabinets, or an odor that returns after seal work. We also review complaints involving leveling jacks that will not deploy, a refrigerator that stops cooling, electrical outlets that lose power, and air conditioning that fails during a trip.

A repeated warning light and a repeated slide-out failure may both mean another service appointment, but the repair history, warranty coverage, and written diagnosis can point in very different directions. We examine each RV complaint as its own record rather than treating every return to the shop as the same problem.

RV case file review

We start a case file review by discussing the RV type, the symptom, the purchase or lease, the warranty, and the number of visits to the dealer or repair facility. That first conversation usually clarifies the basic timeline: what stopped working, whether the defect returned, and whether the RV remained usable between repairs.

We then read the purchase or lease agreement, each repair order, and the dates the RV went into and came out of the shop. We compare the complaint written on the repair order with the technician notes, the parts listed, the mileage or hour-meter entry, and the condition shown when the vehicle was released.

We also gather correspondence with the dealer, manufacturer, warranty administrator, or service manager, including emails, text messages, inspection reports, and appointment confirmations. The paperwork lets us check whether the same fault was reported consistently and whether a repair visit addressed the reported component.

Repair attempts, shop days, warranty

The number of repair attempts matters because the repair orders show whether the shop worked on the same slide-out, engine code, roof leak, leveling jack, or electrical circuit more than once. A visit for a loose cabinet latch does not carry the same weight as repeated visits for a fault that prevents normal operation, so we read the technician description rather than relying only on the service invoice title.

Days out of service also matter. We check the dates in and out of the repair facility, whether parts were on order, and whether the RV could be used while it waited for work. A long shop stay can mean something different from a short visit, and the repair order provides the context.

We check whether the defect was reported while the applicable warranty was running and whether the owner bought or leased the RV. The purchase agreement, warranty booklet, VIN, service records, and correspondence may sit with different people, so we identify who holds each document before drawing conclusions from an incomplete file.

Repair orders and VIN

Before calling, owners can locate the repair orders from the dealer or service center and check whether each one states the same complaint in the customer-concern section. The invoice should show the date the RV arrived, the date it left, the mileage or engine hours, the technician findings, and any parts installed.

We also ask owners to have the purchase or lease agreement available, along with the VIN shown on the registration, insurance card, or driver-side vehicle label. Emails and text messages with the service adviser can help fill gaps where a repair order does not explain a delayed part, a return visit, or a continuing leak.

A simple trip-by-trip timeline can connect each warning light, slide-out failure, or water intrusion report to the corresponding shop date. Call Houston Lemon Law at (832) 245-5128 before the repair record becomes harder to match to the RV's actual condition.
